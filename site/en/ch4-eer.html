The EER model

Enhanced ER (EER) adds the subclass/superclass relationship and inheritance to the ER model. The figures are drawn in the book's notation.

  • Explain subclass / superclass and inheritance with an example; tell specialization and generalization apart.
  • Determine the specialization constraints (disjoint/overlapping, total/partial) from a requirement and draw them correctly.
  • Tell predicate-defined, attribute-defined and user-defined subclasses apart.
  • Compare a shared subclass (lattice) with a category (union type) in terms of membership and inheritance.

Why EER?

In COMPANY all employees are of a single type, EMPLOYEE. Now suppose the company also wants to keep: the typing speed of secretaries, the engineering type of engineers, the pay rate of hourly employees; and that only engineers can be technical leads of projects. With ER there are two bad options:

  • Make them all attributes of EMPLOYEE: Typing_speed stays NULL for everyone who is not a secretary; the rule "only engineers can be technical leads" does not show in the diagram.
  • Create separate entity types SECRETARY, ENGINEER, …: then an engineer and the EMPLOYEE record of the same person look like two separate entities; common attributes such as name and salary are repeated in every type.

EER's answer: ENGINEER is not a separate entity but a subset of EMPLOYEE. It inherits the common properties from EMPLOYEE and adds only its own.

Subclass, superclass, inheritance

  • The entities of an entity type can be divided into meaningful subgroups: among EMPLOYEEs, SECRETARY, ENGINEER, MANAGER, HOURLY_EMPLOYEE… Each group is a subclass, EMPLOYEE their superclass. The relationship between them is IS-A: "a SECRETARY is an EMPLOYEE".
  • An entity in a subclass is the same real-world entity as in the superclass, not a separate entity. Formally, for a subclass S of a superclass C, always S ⊆ C. An entity can be in several subclasses (a salaried engineer is both an ENGINEER and a SALARIED_EMPLOYEE).
  • Type inheritance: a subclass inherits all the attributes and relationships of its superclass. It can also have its own local (specific) attributes (SECRETARY's Typing_speed) and its own specific relationships (BELONGS_TO a trade union that only HOURLY_EMPLOYEEs are members of).

Specialization and generalization

  • Specialization (top-down): dividing the entities of an entity type into subclasses based on a distinguishing characteristic. {SECRETARY, TECHNICIAN, ENGINEER} is a specialization of EMPLOYEE by job type.
  • Generalization (bottom-up): deriving a superclass from entity types that have common attributes. The common attributes of CAR and TRUCK move to VEHICLE.

The two are two design directions of the same construct and are drawn the same way: the superclass is connected to a circle, the circle to the subclasses; the ⊂ on a subclass line shows the direction of the subset. With a single subclass no circle is drawn.

Who decides membership?

KindMembershipExample
Predicate-defined subclassA condition on an attribute of the superclass: S = C[p]. The condition is written on the subclass line.SECRETARY: Job_type = 'Secretary'
Attribute-defined specializationAll the subclasses by conditions on the same attribute (the defining attribute): Si = C[A = ci]. The attribute name is written on the line to the circle.{SECRETARY, TECHNICIAN, ENGINEER} by Job_type
User-defined subclassNo condition; entities are added to the subclass one by one.MANAGER

In predicate- and attribute-defined subclasses the DBMS itself determines membership from the attribute value; in user-defined subclasses insertion and deletion are done by hand.

Constraints on specialization

ConstraintOptionsNotation
DisjointnessDisjoint: an entity is in at most one subclass (Si ∩ Sj = ∅). Overlapping: it can be in several subclasses.d / o in the circle
CompletenessTotal: every entity of the superclass is in at least one subclass (S1 ∪ … ∪ Sn = C). Partial: some are in none.total: a double line from the superclass to the circle

The two constraints are independent; all four combinations are possible. A specialization by a single-valued defining attribute is automatically disjoint; a superclass obtained by generalization is usually total.

Ask two questions of the requirement:

  1. Can an entity be in two subclasses at the same time? Yes → overlapping (o); no → disjoint (d).
  2. Can there be an entity that is in no subclass? Yes → partial (single line); no → total (double line).

EMPLOYEE → {SECRETARY, TECHNICIAN, ENGINEER}: an employee has a single job type (d); there are employees that are none of the three, such as managers (partial). PART → {MANUFACTURED_PART, PURCHASED_PART} (the figure below): a part can be both manufactured and purchased (o); every part is at least one of the two (total).

A double line means two different things. In ER a double line from an entity to a relationship means total participation (every entity takes part in the relationship). In EER a double line from the superclass to the specialization circle means total specialization (every entity is in a subclass). Same symbol, similar idea ("all of them"), but one is about a relationship and the other about subclasses.

  • An entity deleted from the superclass is also deleted from all its subclasses.
  • An entity inserted into the superclass automatically enters the predicate-defined subclasses whose condition it satisfies.
  • In a total specialization an entity inserted into the superclass must also be inserted into at least one subclass.

Hierarchies and lattices

  • Hierarchy: every subclass has a single superclass (a tree, single inheritance).
  • Lattice: a subclass can have several superclasses. Such a shared subclass inherits from all its superclasses (multiple inheritance); an attribute arriving along two paths is inherited once. The classes on the path from the root to a class are its predecessors; a class with no subclasses is a leaf node.

Categories (union types)

The owner of a vehicle can be a person, a bank or a company. OWNER is a subset of the union of different superclasses: T ⊆ (D1 ∪ … ∪ Dn). Such a subclass is called a category. A total category (double line) contains every entity of the union (REGISTERED_VEHICLE = CAR ∪ TRUCK); a partial category need not (not every person owns a vehicle).

Shared subclass (lattice)Category (union type)
MemberIn the intersection of the superclasses: every ENGINEERING_MANAGER is both an ENGINEER and a MANAGER.In the union of the superclasses: every OWNER belongs to only one of PERSON, BANK or COMPANY.
InheritanceFrom all superclasses (multiple inheritance).Only from the superclass it belongs to (selective inheritance).
SuperclassesUsually from the same root, with the same key.Usually different entity types with different keys; that is why a category is often given a surrogate key (Owner_id).
NotationThe subclass is connected to several superclasses with ⊂.The superclasses are connected to a ∪ circle, the circle to the category with ⊂.

A total category can also be modelled as a generalization (VEHICLE as the superclass of CAR and TRUCK). With many common attributes choose a generalization, with few a category.

Design notes

  • A subclass with no attributes or relationships of its own is unnecessary; an attribute in the superclass (e.g. Job_type) is enough.
  • UML equivalents: generalization (a hollow triangle arrow), {disjoint} / {overlapping}, {complete} (total) / {incomplete} (partial).
  • Mapping EER to tables (options 8A–8D and categories) is in Ch 9.2 of the book; not on the midterm.

Test yourself

In a hospital every STAFF member is either a DOCTOR, a NURSE or an ADMINISTRATOR; the same person cannot hold two of these jobs and there are no other staff. How is this specialization drawn?

  1. o in the circle, a double line from the superclass to the circle
  2. d in the circle, a single line from the superclass to the circle
  3. d in the circle, a double line from the superclass to the circle
  4. A category with a ∪ circle
Two questions: can a person be in two subclasses at once? No → d. Can there be staff in no subclass? No → total, a double line.

At a university every person is a student, a staff member or both; people may also be neither (alumni). What are the constraints of the specialization PERSON → {STUDENT, STAFF}?

  1. Disjoint, total
  2. Disjoint, partial
  3. Overlapping, partial
  4. Overlapping, total
"Can be both" → overlapping (o). "May be neither" → partial (a single line).

What is the specialization {SECRETARY, ENGINEER, TECHNICIAN} of EMPLOYEE based on its Job_type attribute?

  1. User-defined
  2. Attribute-defined (and disjoint, since Job_type is single-valued)
  3. A category
  4. A generalization, because three subclasses are gathered in one superclass
All the subclasses are defined by conditions on the same attribute (the defining attribute). Since an employee has a single Job_type, it cannot be in two subclasses at the same time: disjoint.

What is the essential difference between a shared subclass and a category?

  1. A shared subclass has only one superclass; a category has several.
  2. The members of a category are members of all the superclasses.
  3. They are the same; only the notation differs.
  4. A member of a shared subclass is in the intersection of all the superclasses, a member of a category in their union (in only one of them).
An ENGINEERING_MANAGER is both an ENGINEER and a MANAGER and inherits from both. An OWNER is a PERSON, a BANK or a COMPANY and inherits only from the class it belongs to (selective inheritance).

The specialization VEHICLE → {CAR, TRUCK} is disjoint and total. Which of the following is a valid situation?

  1. Every vehicle is either a car or a truck, not both.
  2. Some vehicles are neither cars nor trucks.
  3. A vehicle can be both a car and a truck.
  4. A car does not inherit the attributes of VEHICLE.
Total: VEHICLE = CAR ∪ TRUCK (every vehicle in at least one). Disjoint: CAR ∩ TRUCK = ∅ (in at most one). Together: in exactly one. Inheritance is always there.
